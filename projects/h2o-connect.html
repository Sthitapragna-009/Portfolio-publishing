Homes store it in a rooftop tank and pump it up by hand, with no gauge and no warning.

What people are really trying to do

When the water comes, they want the tank filled without overflowing or running the pump dry, so they can stop checking.

Pushes away from the switch

“Every overflow is water we already paid to pump.”

Answered by: the motor stops at 90% on its own.

Pulls toward H2o

“I want to know without climbing up there.”

Answered by: the level, live, on Home.

Anxiety

“What if it fails while nobody’s watching?”

Answered by: cut-offs on the device and two failure screens.

Habit

“I know the sound it makes when it’s nearly full.”

Answered by: the wall switch always wins.

These are assumptions from desk research, not interviews. People switch when the push and the pull beat the anxiety and the habit.

How it works

A kit that fits the tank, pump and switch a home already has, and an app that tells you what it’s doing.

  1. Sense

    A sonar sensor on the lid reads the level. It never touches the water.

  2. Decide

    The unit by the switch starts the motor at 25% and stops it at 90%, off-peak when it can.

  3. Protect

    If the sump runs dry or the motor overheats, it cuts power in under 200 ms, with or without internet.

  4. Show

    The app shows the level live, keeps a receipt for every run, and stops the motor in one tap.

What it saves

Water that used to run over the top, and power that used to run at the wrong hour.

Water

Never past 90%

The motor stops at 1,800 of 2,000 litres, so the tank doesn't overflow.

  • 42 Llost overnight, flagged by the leak alert the next morning (the app's example)
  • 500 Lis where a refill starts, so the tank never sits empty waiting for someone

Power

20 to 40% lower bills

Estimated for large facilities, from filling only as needed and at off-peak hours.

  • 38%cheaper for tonight's fill, moved to the off-peak tariff (the app's example)
  • <200 msto cut the motor on a dry run, before it burns out

None of this is measured in the homes yet. The study will report real litres and units saved.

Designing for fewer app opens

Most apps want a daily open. This one works when the person who runs the pump stops climbing up to the roof.

Before

A remote for the motor

The controllers on sale could start and stop a pump. None could say what the tank would do next, or prove what it had done.

Decision

A witness that can act.

State and proof come first, controls second. It costs screen space a plain remote wouldn’t need.

What went wrong

Mistakes I caught late, compromises I kept, and what isn’t finished.

  • Changed

    I designed for a valve the kit doesn’t have

    The first leak alert offered to close it. It now pauses automatic filling instead.

  • Redesigned

    My first fix didn’t fix the feel

    Twelve fixes later the screens still looked busy, so I rebuilt the system (decision 5).

  • Kept

    Some numbers don’t add up

    Flow, run times and energy come from different stages. I kept them rather than invent new ones.

  • Not measured

    No study yet

    Every result here is a target, set before the study.

  • Not built

    English and iPhone only

    The homes are in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, where most phones run Android and many people read Telugu.
