Banking concept · iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch · 1 week, solo

Khatiq

Know before it bounces.

Khatiq is a money app for India. It reads your salary, bills and accounts across banks, and tells you three days ahead when a payment is about to bounce. Then it shows calm ways to fix it: move money from savings, pay on salary day, or talk to a person.

For people who can afford their bills, but get caught when rent leaves on the 1st and salary lands on the 3rd.

From khata (खाता), Hindi for an account ledger.

01Overview

The gap, in one picture

Arjun has ₹31,241 today. Rent of ₹32,000 leaves by autopay on 1 Nov; his salary lands on the 3rd. For two days he’s ₹1,058 short, and if the autopay bounces, bank charges and a late fee follow.

Who it’s for

Khatiq starts with one group: salaried people aged 22–35 in Indian cities, paid on a fixed date, with money in two or more banks and three or more autopays (rent, EMIs, SIPs, card dues). They can afford their bills. The dates just don’t line up.

02Challenge

Rent leaves before salary lands

Banking apps show what already happened. The warning arrives as an SMS after the bounce, in words like NACH and mandate. People are left to do the maths themselves.

What already exists

People already get warnings before an autopay. They say a payment is coming, not whether you can cover it.

SignalWhen it arrivesWhat you learn
Pre-debit notice for a card or UPI autopayAbout a day beforeA payment is coming. Not whether your balance covers it.
Low-balance alertWhen the balance drops below a set amountYour balance is low. Not which bill is at risk, or that salary lands in two days.
Bounce SMSAfter the payment failsThat it already failed.
KhatiqThree days beforeHow short, for how long, and three ways out with their costs.

Why now

India’s rails can finally see the whole picture. 338 million accounts are linked through the Account Aggregator, and RBI is making aggregators interoperable by 31 December 2026. UPI e-mandates passed 1.8 billion a month at the top ten banks in July 2026. The data to warn early exists. Nobody yet joins salary dates to debit dates.

Sources: Free Press Journal (new tab) · Inc42 (new tab).

What people need from that moment

Written as jobs to be done. Interviews in India come first in the research plan.

  1. Main job

    Tell me early if I won’t be okay this month.

  2. Emotional job

    Help me feel in control, not judged or alarmed.

  3. Related job

    Show me this is really my bank, and that a person is reachable.

Seven days, five decisions

Emotional design: the right feeling at the right moment

In banking, emotional design isn’t delight. It’s confidence when things are fine, reassurance and control when they aren’t, trust when money moves, and progress after.

The decisions that shaped it

  1. Day 3

    Square, dense components

    Rounded, quiet components

    Sharp cards and heavy lines felt cold for a money worry. Soft shapes and fewer lines felt calmer.

  2. Day 3

    One bank’s home

    A home for all your money

    Most people use two or three banks. The warning belongs above any one of them.

  3. Day 4

    A red “may bounce” banner

    One calm sentence, three ways out

    Say what happens and by how much, then how to fix it.

  4. Day 5

    Two buttons side by side

    One button, one quiet link

    Fix now does the no-fee move. The other ways are a tap away.

  5. Day 6

    Bright colour everywhere

    Black canvas, colour with a job

    Mint for money in, coral for short, lavender for the one action. Colour stays under 10% of any screen.

  6. After review

    An assistant named Seth

    Ask Khatiq

    Seth reads as a moneylender to many Hindi and Gujarati speakers, and as a Western name elsewhere. The assistant became part of Khatiq itself. A name test with speakers across regions is in the research plan.

A review before anyone tests it

Heuristic evaluation, an information-architecture check and a design critique. Eight findings, each fixed. Four of them:

  • Home said too much49 bits of text and five ways to say one fact. Now one sentence and one action.
  • Charts repeated each otherA gauge, bars and rings for one number. Now bars only, and each colour means one thing.
  • Confirm hid the resultIt repeated the amount. Now it shows the new balance and your lowest point before salary.
  • Profile was a tabRarely used, always visible. It moved to the avatar; Scan took the centre of the tab bar.

Heuristics and principles, on the screen

The project’s design principles and Nielsen’s usability heuristics, connected to the parts of the screen where they do their work.

The card on Home

Design principles

  • Warn earlyThree days before rent leaves, not after it bounces.

  • One fact, onceOne button for the quick path, a quiet link for choice.

Heuristics

  • Match the real worldRent, a date and rupees. No NACH, no mandate.

  • Recognition over recallIt says what Fix now will do before you tap.

Confirm the move

Design principles

  • Say it plainlyFrom, to, when, fee. Nothing hidden in fine print.

Heuristics

  • Error preventionThe question repeats the amount and the direction.

  • Visibility of statusYour new balance and lowest point, before money moves.

  • User control and freedomYou can move it back at any time.

Why am I seeing this?

Design principles

  • TransparencyExactly what it looked at to reach the number.

  • Privacy by defaultAnd what it never uses: location, contacts, what you buy.

  • A person is one tap awayFrom the card, the sheet and every error.

Heuristics

  • Help and documentationOne tap explains the warning in a sentence.

When the move fails

Heuristics

  • Recover from errorsWhat happened, and that another way still works.

  • Match the real worldWhy: a ₹44,200 payment left the pot a moment ago.

  • User control and freedomThe no-fee alternative, or a person, right there.

Rent day on the Lock Screen

Design principles

  • Privacy by defaultNo amount until you choose to show it.

  • One fact, onceOne button, and only while there’s something to fix.

Heuristics

  • Visibility of statusRent day, live, without opening the app.

05Impact

What it has to prove

Nothing has been tested yet. These are the bars it must clear with 8 to 10 people, written down now so they can’t drift.

  • Get it in 5 secondsRestate the situation and next step
  • Fix it in 60 secondsCover a gap, unaided
  • Trust it enough to actAnd say where the data came from
  • Find a person in 10 secondsTalk to a person, from the card
  • Feel calm afterOne question, five words

How it gets tested

  1. Interviews10 people (6 salaried, 4 freelancers) about the last time a payment bounced.
  2. Two-week SMS diaryReal bank alerts, and which ones get ignored.
  3. Name and word test20+ people across Hindi, English and South Indian languages, covering Khatiq, the assistant’s name, and words like “short” and “bounce”.
  4. Usability testTwo rounds of five, scored against the targets above.

06Reflection

What I’d keep, and what’s next

  1. Calm is mostly copyThe biggest changes were words. “May bounce” became “you’ll be ₹1,059 short”, and the fix moved into the same card.
  2. Less on home, more on the one thingCutting home from 49 bits of text to a handful made the alert the obvious next step.
  3. Design the wrist firstApple Watch forced the shortest version of every message, and that version improved the phone.
  4. NextThe name and word test, then ten interviews in India, then the card in front of ten people against the targets above. What the research changes will go on this page.
