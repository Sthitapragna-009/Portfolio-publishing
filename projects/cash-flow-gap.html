Banking concept · iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch · 1 week, solo

Khatiq

Know before it bounces.

Khatiq is a money app for India. It reads your salary, bills and accounts across banks, and tells you three days ahead when a payment is about to bounce. Then it shows calm ways to fix it: move money from savings, pay on salary day, or talk to a person.

From khata (खाता), Hindi for an account ledger.

The gap, in one picture

Arjun has ₹31,241 today. Rent of ₹32,000 leaves by autopay on 1 Nov; his salary lands on the 3rd. For two days he’s ₹1,058 short, and the bank charges ₹590 when rent bounces.

02Challenge

Rent leaves before salary lands

Banking apps show what already happened. The warning arrives as an SMS after the bounce, in words like NACH and mandate. People are left to do the maths themselves.

What already exists

People already get warnings before an autopay. They say a payment is coming, not whether you can cover it.

SignalWhen it arrivesWhat you learn
Pre-debit notice for a card or UPI autopayAbout a day beforeA payment is coming. Not whether your balance covers it.
Low-balance alertWhen the balance drops below a set amountYour balance is low. Not which bill is at risk, or that salary lands in two days.
Bounce SMSAfter the payment failsThat it already failed.
KhatiqThree days beforeHow short, for how long, and three ways out with their costs.

What people need from that moment

Written as jobs to be done. Interviews in India come first in the research plan.

  1. Main job

    Tell me early if I won’t be okay this month.

  2. Emotional job

    Help me feel in control, not judged or alarmed.

  3. Related job

    Show me this is really my bank, and that a person is reachable.

Not only rent The same gap shows up for EMIs, credit-card dues, SIPs and freelance pay that lands late. Rent is the clearest example, so the prototype starts there.

03Process

Seven days, five decisions

I mapped the journey, set the emotion each moment needs, brainstormed the ways out, then built and rebuilt the app until every screen said one thing.

Emotional design: the right feeling at the right moment

In banking, emotional design isn’t delight. It’s confidence when things are fine, reassurance and control when they aren’t, trust when money moves, and progress after.

Brainstorm

One idea, six directions. Filled ideas are in the prototype; dashed ones were parked or rejected.

The decisions that shaped it

  1. Day 2

    Square, dense components

    Rounded, quiet components

    Sharp cards and heavy lines felt cold for a money worry. Soft shapes and fewer lines felt calmer.

  2. Day 3

    One bank’s home

    A home for all your money

    Most people use two or three banks. The warning belongs above any one of them.

  3. Day 4

    A red “may bounce” banner

    One calm sentence, three ways out

    Say what happens and by how much, then how to fix it.

  4. Day 5

    A big white button

    A small link

    The card informs. The action is there when you want it.

  5. Day 6

    Bright colour everywhere

    Black canvas, colour only in data

    Colour stays under 10% of any screen, so the one coral number stands out.

A review before anyone tests it

Heuristic evaluation, an information-architecture check and a design critique. Eight findings, each fixed. Four of them:

  • Home said too much49 bits of text and four ways to say one fact. Now one sentence, one gauge, one link.
  • The gauge felt crampedNumber and label fought the ring. Now the number sits alone; the label rests in the opening.
  • Confirm hid the resultIt repeated the amount. Now it shows the new balance and your lowest point before salary.
  • Profile was a tabRarely used, always visible. It moved to the avatar; Scan took the centre of the tab bar.

04Solution

Three days early, three taps to fix

One calm card on the home screen, three ways out with their costs, a confirm that shows the result, and an ending that looks forward.

06Reflection

What I’d keep, and what’s next

  1. Calm is mostly copyThe biggest changes were words. “May bounce” became “you’ll be ₹1,059 short”, and the fix moved into the same card.
  2. Less on home, more on the one thingCutting home from 49 bits of text to a handful made the alert the obvious next step.
  3. Design the wrist firstApple Watch forced the shortest version of every message, and that version improved the phone.
  4. NextFive to eight interviews in India, then the card in front of 8 to 10 people against the targets above.
