Saaf Safar

Airport washrooms that call for help: sensors tell the cleaning crew where to go, in pictures and spoken Hindi

  • For housekeeping crews and their supervisors
  • Modelled on Delhi T3 departures
  • Built a working prototype on a simulated week
Try the prototype Read the journey

Objective

Washrooms in a busy terminal are cleaned on a timetable, while passengers arrive in waves tied to flight banks. Empty tissue dispensers and full bins go unnoticed until a passenger complains or the next round comes by. Off-the-shelf sensors can already report fill levels, supplies, footfall and odour. What is missing is the human layer. Saaf Safar turns those readings into one clear instruction for a worker with mixed literacy, and a live picture for the people who manage them.

Define Stop 07

Principles

Apple’s Human Interface Guidelines, then further wherever they assume a reader. The test for every screen: can a worker who reads Hindi slowly act on it in under five seconds?

  • Pictures
    Pictures first, words secondEvery task and supply has a pictogram; Hindi and English sit under it as support.
  • Heard
    Every task can be heardA speaker button reads the instruction in Hindi, loud enough over cart wheels.
  • Urgency
    Urgency by colour and shapeGo now is red with its own bolt and a pulse, for colour-blind eyes and glare.
  • Gloves
    Targets for gloved handsMain actions are full width and 60 pt tall, above Apple’s 44 pt minimum.
  • Landmarks
    Counts and landmarks, not dataTissue ×4 with a roll, not 16%. Near Gate 14 with the yellow sign workers know.
  • Faces
    Your face is your loginCarts change hands between shifts; sign in once by tapping your own photo.
  • Consent
    Consent for personal devicesPhone alerts are opt-in, never track location, and stop when the shift ends.
  • Configure
    Configure, don’t redesignZones, thresholds and languages are settings per airport, from Delhi to Bidar.

Ideate Stop 10

Lifecycle and escalation

A task moves on by itself when nobody answers. The supervisor is the last step, not the first.

  1. 0 minChime, pictures and spoken Hindi on the nearest cart iPad; phone or watch if the attendant has opted in
  2. 2 minNot taken: offered to the next nearest cart
  3. 5 minStill not taken: the zone dashboard asks the supervisor to call on the walkie-talkie
  4. DoneThe sensor confirms the reset and closes the task. Nobody taps done.

Prototype Stop 13

The cart iPad, step by step

Imran’s evening shift, Cart B3, Zone B. Light on purpose: terminal glare washes out dark screens.

1Start of shift

Tap your photo

The cart shows the zone’s crew for this shift. No password, no typing; the supervisor still sees who took which task.

  • Faces are drawn; every person is fictional

2Alert

Go now, in pictures

A chime, the Go now pill and the gate sign workers already know. Jobs are pictures with counts; the cart checks its own stock and says to stop at the store only when short.

  • 2 minutes to answer before it moves on
  • Listen reads it out in Hindi

If nobody answers in 2 minutes the task moves to the next cart; at 5 minutes the supervisor is asked to radio.

3On the way

Store, then the washroom

A strip of the pier with the route in blue: the store first if needed, then the door. While there, it adds anything nearly empty.

  • Fewer trips: refills come with the first one
  • It knows when you reach the door

4Cleaning

The sensor closes it

Each job fills as time passes. When the levels reset, the sensor closes the task; nobody has to tap done.

5Done

Done, and on to the next

A short confirmation with the time from alert to clean, then back to all clear.

6Language

Hindi first, configurable

Hindi and English, either one first. Other languages are switched on per airport by the admin: Telugu for Hyderabad, Konkani for Goa, Bahasa for Medan.

  • Pictures, signs and colours stay the same everywhere

An Observable notebook behind the thresholds

Why empty a bin at 80% and not 70% or 90%? The notebook re-runs one bin across a day as you move the threshold, the response time and the fixed round, and plots every setting as trips against overflow. Its data loaders run the prototype’s own simulation.

  • Footfall from the timetable
  • The threshold what-if
  • Response by hour and handover
  • Restocking per 100 passengers
