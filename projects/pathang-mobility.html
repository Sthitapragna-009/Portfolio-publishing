Mobility · Product · Service design

Pathang Mobility

A single-occupant autonomous pod for campus commutes

Objective

A single-occupant autonomous pod, and the on-demand service around it, for the one or two kilometre trips a campus runs on, in monsoon rain or summer heat.

Outcomes

  • Mobility design
  • Automotive UX
  • Ride booking app
  • Fleet management
  • Service design
Role
Research, industrial design, fabrication, service and interface design
Context
IIT Roorkee campus
Status
Vehicle built to a working rig · service designed, not deployed

01 The gap

Shared mobility in India is built for the city. On campus the same scooters and rickshaws are rebranded, and nothing is designed for a two kilometre trip taken four times a day.

scooter

Range, but no cover

No weather protection and nowhere secure to leave it.

hoverboard · skateboard

Portable, not practical

Useless on a wet path with a laptop bag.

the opening

A campus is closed, mapped and slow

The conditions under which autonomy works today, and that almost no service is designed around.

02 Who it is for

Four personas from the questionnaire and interviews. None of them want a faster vehicle. They want a trip that is predictable.

Professor

Drives in, because parking is a fight.

Lecturer

Scooters in, and arrives soaked all monsoon.

Assistant

Books autos by phone, and waits past the point of use.

Administrator

Weighs fuel cost against the time a trip takes.

what they share

  • No covered parking
  • No reliable charging
  • Nothing weather protected
  • Options vanish at peak hour

08 Failure identification

The weak points were recorded before the first ride. The folding joint carries the rider’s weight through a hinge, so it is where the design is most exposed.

09 Testing

Ridden on campus roads by people of different heights and builds. It proves the folding delta can carry a person. It doesn’t yet prove range, weather sealing or autonomy.

11 The rider app

Rebuilt after a critique of the first version. A map, one sheet, and one main action per state, from the first sign-in to stepping out at the other end.

Try the prototype (opens in a new tab)

What the first version got wrongSix findings from the review, and what each one became.

first ride

Trust before the first trip

The first version opened straight on a map. Now a short brief shows the bay, the unlock and how to stop, with one thing to try.

unlock

Unlock only when it is there

Unlock was offered while the pod was still two minutes away. It now appears at the bay, and takes a hold, not a tap.

safety

Stop is always in reach

The ride screen led with Change destination. Hold to stop and Help now sit on the phone, the Watch and the Lock Screen.

choice

Pick a type, not a unit

Riders chose between Patang 002 and 009 and read their battery levels. They now choose Lite or Max, and dispatch picks the pod.

pick-up

A numbered bay

The old map never said where the pod would stop. Every ride now names a bay, the walk to it, and the initials on the pod’s light bar.

fare

One fixed fare

“₹10 so far” sat beside “fare is fixed”. The fare is shown once, before the request, and it does not change.

  4. Running lateIt asks before it acts
The assistantNo permanent bar. It speaks three times in a ride, and says why each time.

before

It suggests the usual trip

From the timetable: Studio at 9:00, leave by 8:45. One tap books it; ignoring it costs nothing.

waiting

It explains a delay

A cart is blocking Library Road, so the pod took the east loop. One minute longer, said in one line.

at the bay

It offers to hold the pod

Eighty metres away with 48 seconds left, it offers two more minutes. The rider decides.
