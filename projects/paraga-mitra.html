Objective

            

              
Farmers need bees during a bloom that lasts a few weeks. Migratory beekeepers need a string of those blooms across a year. Today the two find each other through phone calls and favours. Paraga Mitra is the service, and the system under it, that books the bees to the bloom, keeps the colonies safe while they work, and pays both sides on time. It is built for people who may not read easily.

01  The gap

          
Bee pollination is one of the cheapest yield inputs a farmer can buy. Yet outside one state in India, nobody sells it as a product. It is traded on memory, phone calls and trust.

        


        

          

            

              
    
                
  • +47%mustard yield with 3 hives of Apis mellifera a hectare, in on-farm trials. About 40% with 5 hives of Apis cerana.From ICAR trials · JISCAR 2022
    • 
                
  • 14–20×the value of the extra fruit from pollination, compared with the honey the same colonies give.From Govt. of Himachal Pradesh
    • 
                
  • ₹1.2–2ka month to rent one box for April flower set in Himachal, the only organised rental market in the region.From Down To Earth · ICIMOD
    • 
              

              

              
    
                
  • Pesticide spraysWhole colonies lost in a night
    • 
                
  • Bloom timingNobody publishes when fields flower
    • 
                
  • Heat and coldFewer honey days, sudden losses
    • 
                
  • MiddlemenBetween keepers and honey buyers
    • 
              

            

          

          
the opening
The value is clear. The market isn’t.
The farmer gains the most and carries the least risk. The beekeeper risks the colonies and is paid last. A neighbour’s spray can wipe out both sides’ work in a night. Nobody owns the handshake between them.

          
what already exists
  • NBHM: training for migratory beekeeping
  • Madhukranti: beekeeper registry and honey traceability
  • BeeConnected (UK, Canada, Australia): spray alerts
  • Humble Bee: pollination module planned
Registries, subsidies and alerts each exist on their own. Nothing joins them into a booking a farmer can make.

        


        
        

          
02  Field visits and desk study

          

            
The project ran on desk research, grounded by visits to beekeepers at their camps. Those were informal conversations to check the desk findings against the boxes, the trucks and the people. They were not a structured interview study, and nothing on this page is quoted from them.

  3. Migratory economics (PAU, 210 beekeepers)
  4. Pesticide losses and decline
  5. Existing platforms, India and abroad

documented pain points
  • Pesticide poisoning
  • Forage dearth forces migration
  • Heat waves, fewer honey days
  • Unseasonal cold killing colonies
  • Middlemen in honey sales
Scroll · Down To Earth · AP · LRRD · Indian J. Entomology

          
hypotheses still to test in the field
  • Late or haggled payment
  • Hive-count disputes
  • Trucks hard to book at short notice
  • Farmers can’t judge colony strength
Plausible from the documented picture, but not confirmed. They are written into the design as risks, not facts.

Four archetypes built from the research, not from interviews. Two of them never meet without someone in between.

        


        

          
archetype 01

Three hundred boxes, four or five moves a year, a route planned on memory and calls.
needs · a full route, safe sites

          
archetype 02

Mustard on four acres. Knows bees help, but not how many hives, or when. May not read comfortably.

06  Insights to principles

          
Six things the research kept pointing at, each turned into a rule the service has to keep.

        


        

          
01
The bloom is the deadline, not the date
Book against a forecast bloom window per field, and move the window when the weather does.

          
02
The value sits with the farmer, the risk with the beekeeper
Price the risk in: escrow up front, spray clauses, compensation drawn from a deposit.

          
03
One spray can erase a season
Spray safety is part of the core service, not a feature. Every placement sits inside a geofence.

          
04
Trust is made in the field
A trained agent from the FPO or KVK verifies the hive count and colony strength at placement.

          
05
Reading is optional
Voice, icons and counted objects carry every screen. Missed-call IVR and agents cover people without smartphones.

          
06
Native bees are stakeholders too
Cap hive density per area, and recommend Apis cerana where local beekeepers already keep it.

        


        
        

          
07  The service

          
Six offerings, with one account for each farmer and each beekeeper, and field agents and state departments plugged in. The farmer pays for bees per hive, per bloom. Everything else is there to make that booking safe.

        


        

          
for farmers
Pollination booking
Hives sized to the field (3 per hectare for mustard with A. mellifera), placed before 10% bloom.

          
for beekeepers
Route planner
Bloom forecasts along the route, with unbooked gaps shown as open capacity.

          
for both
Spray-safe network
Farmers and input dealers log sprays. Apiaries nearby are warned in time to act.

          
for both · field agent
Placement check
Count, tag, open one box at random, photograph. The check releases the first payment.

          
for both
Escrow and payouts
Half at placement, half at bloom end, over UPI. No cash and no haggling.

          
for beekeepers
Honey passport
Each batch is linked to the beekeeper’s Madhukranti ID and the crop it came from, for buyers who pay for provenance.

10  The spray-safety network

          
The riskiest moment in the journey gets its own loop. BeeConnected already proves the model in three countries. Paraga Mitra adds input dealers, voice calls, and consequences written into the contract.

        


        

          

            
1A spray is logged
By the farmer in the app or by IVR, or by the input dealer at the point of sale. Product type and time.

            
2Geofence check
Every apiary within 3 km of the field, adjustable to 5 km like BeeConnected. Contracted host fields are checked against their spray clause.

            
3Beekeepers warned
A push alert, then a voice call in their language, at least 48 hours ahead. Locations of other apiaries stay private.

            
4They act
Close entrances overnight, ask the farmer for a delay, or move the hives. One tap each.

            
5The outcome is kept
An unannounced spray near a placement counts against the deposit and the rating. Good records lower a farmer’s price next season.

          

        


        
        

          
11  Designing for low literacy

          
Many of the farmers and beekeepers this serves read slowly or not at all, and some read Hindi or Punjabi but not English. The apps follow the twelve guidelines in Guimarães et al.’s review of interface design for low-literate users (ICEEL 2022). Each one is turned into a specific decision on screen.

        


        

          

            
    
              
  • One job per screenEach screen asks one question. Everything else waits for the next one.minimal design · essential information
    • 
              
  • Shape, not just colourDanger changes the card’s shape and adds an icon. The review notes that colour alone is too subtle.visual cues · error messages
    • 
              
  • Recognise, don’t recallThree tabs that never hide. Choices are tiles to tap, never fields to type.recognition · writing ability
    • 
              
  • The words people use“Bee boxes”, not “colonies”. “Insect spray”, not “insecticide”.clear language
    • 
              
  • A straight lineBooking runs as three steps in a line, never a menu inside a menu.simple navigation
    • 
              
  • Always know where you are“Step 2 of 3”, a back button that says where it goes, and a status on every booking.system status
    • 
              
  • Errors say what to do“Not insect spray while bees are here. It kills them. Ask the shop for a bee-safe one.”simple error messages
    • 
              
  • Help is a voiceA speaker on every screen reads it aloud. A call button reaches a person.help
    • 
              
  • Counted, not just numberedFive boxes are drawn as five boxes. Money is split into the two moments it moves.beyond the review · numeracy
    • 
              
  • Drawn, not photographedSimple drawings of things from the farm. The review cites findings that photos carry too much detail, and abstract icons too little.after Medhi Thies, via the review
    •

15  AI in Paraga Mitra

          
AI does the forecasting, matching and checking that no one has time for. People make every decision that moves money or bees. Behind the scenes it automates the work. On screen it lets someone who can’t read the interface still use the service.

        


        

          

            
AI for automation · backstage

            
    
              
  • Bloom forecastingA bloom window for every field, from crop, sowing date, district weather and satellite greenness, with a stated confidence.crop model · IMD · Sentinel-2
    • 
              
  • Matching and route planningFills each keeper’s hive-weeks along a route and keeps truck kilometres down, within the hard rules on species and density.constraint solver + learned fit score
    • 
              
  • Colony strength from a photoEstimates comb coverage on one frame at the placement check, so “strong” means the same thing on every farm.vision model · agent confirms
    • 
              
  • Spray riskSpots likely spray days from dealer sales, crop stage and weather, and warns keepers before anyone has logged a spray.risk model · early warning
    • 
              
  • Dispute triage and anomaliesGathers the evidence for an agent: photos, tags, timelines, past incidents. Flags patterns like the same count gap appearing season after season.summaries · anomaly detection
    • 
            

          

          

            
AI for experience · frontstage

            
    
              
  • Ask Mitra, by voiceQuestions in Hindi, Punjabi or Bhojpuri, answered aloud from the farmer’s own bookings, never from guesswork.Bhashini speech + grounded answers
    • 
              
  • Every screen read aloudIncluding the agreement, summarised into one minute of plain speech.text to speech · summarising
    • 
              
  • Proactive nudges“Flowers are coming early. Move the bees?” One question, at the right time, with a yes and a no.forecast → plain language
    • 
              
  • Smart defaultsThe field from the land record, the crop from last season, the route from the forecasts. Fewer questions to answer.AgriStack · history
    • 
              
  • Ops copilotIn the dashboard, answers questions like “which placements are at risk this week?” with the records behind them, and drafts the fix for a person to approve.retrieval over bookings · drafts, not actions
    •

18  Outcomes and the pilot

          
What success would be measured on, and a rollout that starts where a rental market already exists. The targets are hypotheses to test, not results.

        


        

          
for farmers
Bloom coverage
Share of booked hectares with hives in place before 10% bloom.
target · 90%+

          
for colonies
Spray incidents
Unannounced sprays near placements, per 100 bookings.
target · falling each season

          
for beekeepers
Days to payment
From the end of bloom to money in the account.
target · 2 days

          
for low-literate users
Booked without help
Share of farmers who complete a booking by app or IVR, without an agent.
baseline unknown · measure first

          
for the service
Repeat bookings
Farmers who book the same or a better-rated beekeeper next season.
baseline unknown · measure first

          
pilot · three phases, one route
  1. Himachal apple, with the horticulture department
  2. Bihar litchi, through FPOs and the KVK
  3. Rajasthan and Punjab mustard, with input dealers
Start by digitising a market that already pays. Then add the agent network. Then take on the hardest part, field crops with heavy spraying.

        


        
        

          
19  Limits and next steps

          
What this project doesn’t know yet, said plainly.

        


        

          
limits
  • Desk research plus informal visits. Not a structured study
  • Yield gains are from specific trials and won’t hold everywhere
  • Prices in the prototype are illustrative
  • The low-literacy guidelines come from a review, not from tests with these users
  • Effects on native bees need an ecologist, not a designer

          
next
  • Structured interviews along one route, both sides
  • Test the icons and voice flow with low-literate farmers
  • Test a light mode against the dark one in direct sun
  • Co-design the spray protocol with input dealers
  • Run a paper pilot of escrow with one FPO
