Objective

Farmers need bees during a bloom that lasts a few weeks. Migratory beekeepers need a string of those blooms across a year. Today the two find each other through phone calls and favours. Paraga Mitra is the service, and the system under it, that books the bees to the bloom, keeps the colonies safe while they work, and pays both sides on time. It is built for people who may not read easily.

The gap

Bee pollination is one of the cheapest yield inputs a farmer can buy. Yet outside one state in India, nobody sells it as a product. It is traded on memory, phone calls and trust.

  • +47%mustard yield with 3 hives of Apis mellifera a hectare, in on-farm trials. About 40% with 5 hives of Apis cerana.From ICAR trials · JISCAR 2022
  • 14–20×the value of the extra fruit from pollination, compared with the honey the same colonies give.From Govt. of Himachal Pradesh
  • ₹1.2–2ka month to rent one box for April flower set in Himachal, the only organised rental market in the region.From Down To Earth · ICIMOD
  • Pesticide spraysWhole colonies lost in a night
  • Bloom timingNobody publishes when fields flower
  • Heat and coldFewer honey days, sudden losses
  • MiddlemenBetween keepers and honey buyers

the opening

The value is clear. The market isn’t.

The farmer gains the most and carries the least risk. The beekeeper risks the colonies and is paid last. A neighbour’s spray can wipe out both sides’ work in a night. Nobody owns the handshake between them.

what already exists

  • NBHM: training for migratory beekeeping
  • Madhukranti: beekeeper registry and honey traceability
  • BeeConnected (UK, Canada, Australia): spray alerts
  • Humble Bee: pollination module planned

Registries, subsidies and alerts each exist on their own. Nothing joins them into a booking a farmer can make.

problem statement

Farmers need bees for a few weeks of bloom; migratory beekeepers need a string of blooms across the year. They have no reliable, trusted way to find each other, agree terms, keep the colonies safe from sprays and settle up, and many of them can’t use a text-heavy app.

how might we

HMW book bees against a bloom that moves, not a fixed date?

HMW let both sides trust a deal they can’t fully see or read?

HMW warn a beekeeper before a spray, not after it?

HMW make every step work without reading?

HMW reach a farmer who has no smartphone?added after the first field reactions

Insights to principles

Six things the research kept pointing at, each turned into a rule the service has to keep.

01

The bloom is the deadline, not the date

Book against a forecast bloom window per field, and move the window when the weather does.

02

The value sits with the farmer, the risk with the beekeeper

Price the risk in: escrow up front, spray clauses, compensation drawn from a deposit.

03

One spray can erase a season

Spray safety is part of the core service, not a feature. Every placement sits inside a geofence.

04

Trust is made in the field

A trained agent from the FPO or KVK verifies the hive count and colony strength at placement.

05

Reading is optional

Voice, icons and counted objects carry every screen. Missed-call IVR and agents cover people without smartphones.

06

Native bees are stakeholders too

Cap hive density per area, and recommend Apis cerana where local beekeepers already keep it.

The service

Six offerings, with one account for each farmer and each beekeeper, and field agents and state departments plugged in. The farmer pays for bees per hive, per bloom. Everything else is there to make that booking safe.

for farmers

Pollination booking

Hives sized to the field (3 per hectare for mustard with A. mellifera), placed before 10% bloom.

for beekeepers

Route planner

Bloom forecasts along the route, with unbooked gaps shown as open capacity.

for both

Spray-safe network

Farmers and input dealers log sprays. Apiaries nearby are warned in time to act.

for both · field agent

Placement check

Count, tag, open one box at random, photograph. The check releases the first payment.

for both

Escrow and payouts

Half at placement, half at bloom end, over UPI. No cash and no haggling.

for beekeepers

Honey passport

Each batch is linked to the beekeeper’s Madhukranti ID and the crop it came from, for buyers who pay for provenance.

the booking lifecycle · money moves on the yellow states

System design

Five channels feed one set of services. Location is the core data type: fields are polygons and apiaries are points. Almost every rule is a distance check: matching, spray alerts and density caps. The apps are built to work offline, because most placements happen where the signal doesn’t reach.

matching, in one line

Hard rules first, then a score.

  1. Hives needed = hectares × the crop’s rate
  2. Drop keepers whose route misses the bloom window
  3. Drop species not allowed for that region
  4. Score: window overlap, detour, colony strength, rating
  5. Show three. A field agent can override.

The spray-safety network

The riskiest moment in the journey gets its own loop. BeeConnected already proves the model in three countries. Paraga Mitra adds input dealers, voice calls, and consequences written into the contract.

1A spray is logged

By the farmer in the app or by IVR, or by the input dealer at the point of sale. Product type and time.

2Geofence check

Every apiary within 3 km of the field, adjustable to 5 km like BeeConnected. Contracted host fields are checked against their spray clause.

3Beekeepers warned

A push alert, then a voice call in their language, at least 48 hours ahead. Locations of other apiaries stay private.

4They act

Close entrances overnight, ask the farmer for a delay, or move the hives. One tap each.

5The outcome is kept

An unannounced spray near a placement counts against the deposit and the rating. Good records lower a farmer’s price next season.

Designing for low literacy

Many of the farmers and beekeepers this serves read slowly or not at all, and some read Hindi or Punjabi but not English. The apps follow the twelve guidelines in Guimarães et al.’s review of interface design for low-literate users (ICEEL 2022). Each one is turned into a specific decision on screen.

  • One job per screenEach screen asks one question. Everything else waits for the next one.minimal design · essential information
  • Shape, not just colourDanger changes the card’s shape and adds an icon. The review notes that colour alone is too subtle.visual cues · error messages
  • Recognise, don’t recallThree tabs that never hide. Choices are tiles to tap, never fields to type.recognition · writing ability
  • The words people use“Bee boxes”, not “colonies”. “Insect spray”, not “insecticide”.clear language

what this round can and can’t say

It found problems, quickly and cheaply, and it changed the design. It can’t say how often each problem happens, or whether the fixes work for farmers who read little. That needs a planned study with them, in the next section.

The hallway tests were with people who read easily. The real question is which cue a farmer who reads little actually uses: the picture, the word, the number or the voice. Eye tracking shows where people look before they act, without asking them to explain it in words.

Who
8 to 12 farmers who read little, recruited through the KVK, with an interpreter and spoken consent
How
Mobile eye-tracking glasses on a real phone, in their own village, then a short talk-through of the replay
Tasks
Find when the bees arrive · book bee boxes · answer a spray alert
Areas
A the bloom · B the boxes · C the yellow button · D the read-aloud button
Measures
Time to the first look at C, looks at icons against words, how often D is found, task success, and the path the eyes take on the alert
Decides
Whether labels can shrink, whether voice should start by itself, and where the button belongs
